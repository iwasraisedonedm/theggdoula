A birth doula offers continuous, non-medical support during labor and birth. My work begins before labor: we have time to get to know each other, talk about what matters to you, and consider how you would like support to feel. You can bring questions as they come up without needing to have every decision made in advance.

## What support can look like

During labor, I can offer reassurance, help you try comfort measures, and support communication between you, your partner or chosen support people, and your clinical team. I can help you remember questions you want to ask and keep your preferences in view as plans change. Your licensed clinicians remain responsible for medical assessment, recommendations, and care.

Your choices are personal. Some people want to discuss medication options; others have different preferences or are still deciding. A doula does not prescribe one kind of birth or make decisions for you.

## What a doula does not do

I do not provide medical care, diagnose, interpret clinical findings, or replace your doctor or midwife. If a medical concern comes up, contact your clinical team. My role is to provide non-medical emotional, informational, and physical support while you receive care from qualified health professionals.

The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists describes continuous one-to-one emotional support from trained support personnel, including doulas, as an addition to regular nursing care. This does not guarantee a particular labor or birth outcome; each experience is individual. [Read ACOG’s guidance on approaches to limit intervention during labor and birth](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2019/02/approaches-to-limit-intervention-during-labor-and-birth).
