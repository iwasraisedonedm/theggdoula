Your partner does not need to know exactly what to do before labor begins. You can prepare together by talking about what support feels helpful, how each of you hopes to participate, and what you might do if plans change.

## Before labor

Consider discussing a few practical questions together:

- What helps you feel listened to and supported?
- Would you like your partner to offer comfort measures, help you communicate, or take care of practical details?
- How do you want support offered if you are tired or focused inward?
- Who should be contacted or updated, and what information are you comfortable sharing?

These are conversation prompts, not a checklist you have to complete. Your preferences are yours, and they can change.

## During labor

A doula can help your partner find ways to participate, offer reassurance, and keep your preferences in view. I do not replace your partner or direct your clinical care. Your partner can remain close, take breaks, or ask me for practical suggestions based on what you have discussed.

You can include your partner in prenatal planning and talk through your preferred roles during a [free consultation](/book/). Learn more about [birth doula support](/services/birth/).

The [World Health Organization’s evidence brief on labor companionship](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-SRH-20.13) discusses support from a chosen companion during labor and birth.
